About us

Connecting mechanics, materials and intelligent structural engineering.

The Structural Mechanics & Sustainable Structures (SMS) Research Group brings together researchers working across reinforced-concrete structures, advanced construction materials, structural strengthening, durability, computational mechanics and artificial intelligence.

Our work combines experimental investigation, analytical modelling, nonlinear finite-element analysis, database development and machine learning to address practical challenges in structural engineering.

Research-driven
From fundamental mechanics to practical engineering applications.
Interdisciplinary
Structural engineering, sustainable materials, durability and AI/ML.
Open to collaboration
We welcome academic, industry and student collaboration.
Research areas

Research for resilient and sustainable infrastructure

Our activities span structural mechanics, materials, durability, strengthening, earthquake engineering and data-driven modelling.

Green & low-carbon materials

Development and application of environmentally responsible materials for structural engineering.

High-performance & smart materials

Advanced cementitious and composite materials with enhanced structural performance and functionality.

Durability of RC structures

Corrosion, degradation and service-life assessment of reinforced-concrete structures in aggressive environments.

Strengthening & composite systems

FRP, FRCM/TRM and sustainable strengthening solutions for concrete and masonry structures.

Shells, plates & tensegrity

Mechanics, modelling and innovative design of plates, shells and lightweight structural systems.

Earthquake engineering

Nonlinear response, performance-based assessment and seismic resilience of structural systems.

AI & machine learning

Data-driven prediction, explainable AI and hybrid mechanics–ML approaches for structures and materials.

RC structural behavior

Shear, punching shear, flexure, bond and nonlinear behavior of reinforced-concrete members and connections.

Computational mechanics

Analytical modelling, nonlinear finite-element analysis and computational tools for structural assessment.
